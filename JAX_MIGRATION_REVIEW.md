# PAWN v1→v2 (PyTorch→JAX) Migration Review — Final Report

## 1. Verdict

The v2 JAX/Equinox/Optax migration is **mechanically sound at its core and well-engineered in its hot paths, but its periphery has drifted out of sync with its own documented contracts**. The model core, supernet slicing arithmetic, KV-cache decode, JAX discipline (PRNG, donation, scan hygiene, dtype policy), and checkpoint integrity layer are all correct and, in several places, verified against the v1 reference. Efficiency posture is strong: the K-step `lax.scan` pretrain loop, fused cross-entropy, bf16 optimizer state, length bucketing, and Pallas-flash attention are real, benchmark-grounded wins. The recurring failure mode is **inert configuration and silently-dropped capabilities**: a cluster of documented knobs (`prepend_outcome`, `mate_boost`, `max_grad_norm`, `epochs`/`steps_per_epoch`/`data_seed`/`val_every`, `--wandb`) are validated by pydantic but never consumed, and several v1 features (`schedule_health.json`, real linear probes, RNG/scheduler persistence) were lost without updating the docs that still advertise them. None of the confirmed findings is a crash-on-happy-path or data-corruption critical; the highs are silent miscalibrations, leaked val splits, an off-by-one eval, and entirely non-functional sweep strategies. **Net: a correct training engine wrapped in a config/eval/observability surface that overstates what it actually does.**

## 4. Architectural Assessment

### Supernet soundness — **sound with caveats**
The sliced shared-weight supernet is a mechanically correct, textbook MatFormer/slimmable implementation.
- **head_dim=64 invariance (fully sound):** `d_model == head_dim·n_heads` is a ModelConfig invariant; slicing `[:dv]` keeps whole 64-channel heads byte-for-byte; RoPE phases are head-local and slice-invariant. Attention is mathematically identical per-head across slices.
- **Slice usability (mostly sound):** RMSNorm self-normalizes over the actual `d_V` axis; all field slices are dimensionally coherent. Depth-slicing is currently a no-op (all production variants are 10 layers) — good, it sidesteps MatFormer's known depth-elastic weakness, but the depth-slice machinery is therefore untested in the field.
- **The real caveat — gradient tug-of-war:** every variant's gradient flows into the *same* top-left weight block (`trainer.py:281-291`). This is maximal coupling vs v1 cotrain's zero coupling (three independent optimizers). There is **no gradient balancing** (no PCGrad, no per-variant weighting, no normalization) and **no quality-parity guard** (tests assert slices *run*, never that `small`-v2 ≈ an independently-trained d=256). The literature predicts the smallest slice pays a measurable accuracy tax — and that is exactly the artifact most used in a "small-scale testbed," producing a confound (adapter results partly measure "method repairs supernet interference"). The SUM-of-CEs choice's provenance comment is also wrong (v1 cotrain summed *nothing* into shared weights). **Verdict: a defensible, arguably best-fit primary design for the ROCm-single-GPU constraint, but `small`-v2 should not be trusted as a clean baseline without an empirical parity check.**

### Sampling unbiasedness — **the claim genuinely holds**
The stochastic-sandwich estimator `loss = CE_supernet + N·CE_sampled` with uniform `P(i)=1/N` and multiplier `N` is a **correct single-sample Monte-Carlo estimator of the exhaustive sum, for both loss and gradient** (grad is linear; the supernet term is deterministic; all variants share the same loss-mask denominator, so no normalization trap). The PRNG split is correct and decorrelated (`fold_in(state.key, state.step)`, step is the scan carry; per-micro `fold_in` in the accumulation path). **Important nuance:** always-including the supernet does **not** bias toward the largest variant — it is unbiased — but it creates a **variance asymmetry**: the supernet gets a zero-extra-variance full gradient every step while small/base get an N×-magnitude signal on 1/N of steps (~2× variance inflation at N=2), which can slow small/base convergence under a fixed LR. The one real documentation defect: the "~50-67% FLOP savings" headline is the asymptotic many-variant figure; the measured production win is **~10%** (the always-on d=640 supernet dominates compute). The default `stochastic_variants=True` is justified, but the docstring should be reconciled.

### JAX discipline — **sound**
A clean sweep: PRNG fold-in idioms correct everywhere; donation correct at the outer scan boundary (inner `train_step` donation is a harmless dead directive); scan carries are shape/dtype-invariant with no host callbacks; dtype policy is consistent and correct (fp32 RMSNorm reduction, fp32 softmax intermediate, fp32 logsumexp in the fused loss, bf16 first-moment / fp32 second-moment optimizer); adapter freezing via grad-arg partition correctly DCEs backbone grads; no tracer leaks or in-jit side effects (the RoSA host-side top-k is a documented, correct ROCm escape). Only minor multi-compile spots (bucket-shape variety, ragged tail batches) — bounded and amortized by the persistent compile cache.

### Efficiency posture — **sound with caveats**
The hot paths are well-engineered and benchmark-grounded: K-step `lax.scan` pretrain loop, fused CE (avoids materializing `(B,T,V)` log-probs), lazy DCE'd masks, bf16 optimizer state, branchless clip, length bucketing (empirically convergence-neutral), and **Pallas-flash as the actual GPU training default** (CLAUDE.md's "materialised QK^T default" is *stale* — plain QK^T is only the CPU/parity baseline, and it correctly is, since it materializes a `(B,H,T,T)` tensor + fp32 copy, non-viable at training batch). KV-cache decode is correctly wired and bit-parity tested. **Caveats:** (a) grad accumulation is C.4-complete + tested but unreachable from the production entry point (prefetcher shape gap); (b) remat is env-gated (`PAWN_USE_REMAT`) not a run_config field, so the documented B=256 unlock is undiscoverable on cloud launches; (c) the Pallas-flash custom_vjp backward is the single load-bearing correctness dependency of the whole training-shape memory story and has **no committed numeric gradient-parity test** (unlike the use_sdpa path's 2.4e-7 check); (d) the adapter loop's missing scan (H11) is a real regression against the subsystem's own pretrain design.

## 5. Alternate Designs Worth Considering

Ranked by relevance to PAWN's testbed-at-small-scale goal:

1. **Add a quality-parity regression harness (no architecture change).** Slice `small` from the trained supernet and compare per-phase move accuracy against a from-scratch solo-trained d=256 on identical data/steps; bake the gap into `schedule_health.json` or a bench. *Highest-value, zero-risk addition — it empirically validates the entire supernet bet and arguably should gate publishing the v2 `small` checkpoint.*
2. **Add gradient balancing to the supernet (PCGrad / per-variant loss weights).** Directly targets the one real risk (tug-of-war on the small slice). PCGrad needs per-variant grads materialized separately (memory cost, erodes some DCE win); loss-weighting is cheap but needs tuning. Pairs naturally with #1 to know it worked.
3. **Finish wiring grad accumulation through the prefetcher** (emit `(K,N,B,T)`). Pure data-loop plumbing — the compute kernel is done and tested. Unlocks effective-batch scaling that is currently dead code.
4. **Promote remat to a run_config field** (default-on at LARGE/high-batch via a VRAM heuristic). Makes the documented top memory lever reachable from configs and cloud launches.
5. **Distillation to small/base from the frozen supernet.** Decouples the small model's optimization from the shared-block compromise while keeping a single primary training run; gives a third testbed axis (distilled vs from-scratch vs slice). More moving parts than PCGrad.
6. *(Lower relevance)* **Independent models sharing only the data pipeline** (v1's scheme, kept lean): cleanest baselines, but 3× wall-clock and throws away the migration's central bet — only justified if #1 reveals an unacceptable tax. **Sample the supernet too / LoRA-only variant family / structured pruning** are not worth it for this goal (respectively: injects variance into the most-valued variant; discards the scale axis; a lateral move with as much machinery as the supernet).

## 6. Themes & Recommendations

**Cross-cutting themes:**
- **Validated-but-inert config knobs.** The dominant pattern: pydantic accepts and validates fields that no trainer consumes — `prepend_outcome` (H1), `mate_boost`, `max_grad_norm` (H10), `epochs`/`steps_per_epoch`/`data_seed`/`val_every`, `accumulation_steps`, `--wandb`. These give a false sense of control and, worse, silently change/corrupt behavior (H1, H10). **Every config field should either be consumed or rejected at parse time.**
- **Silently-dropped v1 capabilities with stale docs still advertising them.** `schedule_health.json` (H7), real linear probes (H6), RNG/scheduler persistence, FiLM semantics (H2), adapter-param resume (H3), wandb. CLAUDE.md and module docstrings are the canonical orientation map and currently overstate what ships.
- **Tests that assert shape/identity-at-init but never the trained math.** H2 (FiLM), H5 (edge-case off-by-one), H6 (noise probes), H8 (sweep argv), the tautological PAD-init test, and the untested bf16-μ round-trip all slipped through because tests check structure, not behavior.
- **Eval surface doesn't read the checkpoint's own training config** (`prepend_outcome` in both eval paths), producing silently-wrong numbers for outcome-conditioned models.
- **The core is trustworthy; the periphery is not.** Model, supernet math, JAX discipline, and checkpoint integrity are verified-correct. Config plumbing, eval, sweep, and observability are where the defects cluster.

**Prioritised next steps:**
